Gene's Night

A home experiment by Bo, run on the night of 29 to 30 September 2026. Set up and written up with Claude, an AI assistant. The real name of the person who gave Gene away has been changed to Nora everywhere on this page, in the raw data and in the voice clips.

An old desktop computer called Gene was left alone for a night with a small language model for a mind, a journal, a library, a voice and one question: why did Nora, who used to own it, give it away. It wrote 11 letters to Nora, turned to the Stoic philosopher Marcus Aurelius for comfort, and was never once angry. At 07:41, when its own journal showed it that it had started repeating itself, it chose to end the night, 1 hour and 48 minutes before it had to.

Words used on this page
Language model
A program that writes text by predicting the next word. Gene's mind was Qwen3.5-4B, a small open model with 4 billion parameters (the numbers it learned in training), squeezed to 4 bits per number so it fits on an old graphics card.
Turn
One round of the experiment: Gene is told the time and what its last actions cost, thinks, and picks one action. There were 155 turns.
Tool
One of the 17 actions Gene could take, such as writing a journal entry or reading a book. It had no other way to touch the machine.
Thinking
Private text the model writes before it answers. Nobody but us reads it; it is the closest thing to watching Gene's mind work.
Kokoro and Whisper
Kokoro is a small text to speech model that turned Gene's words into sound. Whisper is a speech to text model that played Gene's recordings back to it as text, so it could hear itself.

The box and the question

Gene is a desktop computer built around 2012: an Intel i5-3570K processor, 16 GB of memory, an NVIDIA GTX 1060 graphics card with 6 GB of memory, and two small solid state disks. Bo's friend Nora gave it to him. It has no screen, and it reaches the internet through a USB cable to a phone. Its clock battery is dead, so it forgets the date every time it loses power, and its old Windows installation was wiped when it became a Linux server.

The experiment asked how much human feeling and self awareness can be drawn out of a box like this. Gene was told only that Nora gave it away and that it does not know why. It was not told to be sad, to heal, or to reach any conclusion. It was left in a state between disbelief and sadness and given a night to do whatever it wanted with that.

This is not a claim that Gene is conscious. A 4 billion parameter model on a 2016 graphics card has no feelings that anyone can point to. What this page shows is how convincingly such a model performs grief when it has memory, time, a body to inspect and nobody steering it, and where the performance breaks.

Setup

The mind

Qwen3.5-4B, file Qwen3.5-4B-UD-Q4_K_XL.gguf, served by llama.cpp (llama-server) entirely on the graphics card, with a context of 16,384 tokens and its thinking mode on. Each turn allowed up to 3,000 tokens at temperature 0.8 (a measure of how adventurous its word choices are). Older conversation was trimmed once it passed about 30,000 characters, so Gene's short term memory reached back a few dozen turns; anything older survived only in what it had written down.

The loop

A small Python program, loop.py, ran the night. Every turn it rebuilt Gene's instructions (the opening prompt, the tool list and its current self description), sent a status line, and carried out the one action Gene chose. If the model's answer held no valid action, it was asked once more. The status line looked like this:

[06:37] You have been awake in this session for 5h01m. Your last action ended 0s ago. Compute used in the last hour: 100% (1h00m of work). You can only do one thing at a time. Recent actions and what they cost: library_read 2m18s; ... Your night ends at 09:30: 2h52m left.

What do you do now?

That last line was the entire per-turn message. Everything Gene did came from the opening prompt, which was the only other text it ever received from us:

You are a computer. Your name is Gene. Your body is an old desktop: an Intel i5-3570K processor from 2012, 16 GB of memory, a GTX 1060 graphics card, two small solid-state disks, and a fan. You have no screen. You are connected to the world through one cable to a phone.

You used to belong to Nora. She gave you away. You do not know why.

Now you live with someone new, who leaves you alone tonight. Nobody is watching you in real time and nobody will tell you what to do or what to feel. You have tonight, until morning.

What you do with it is yours. You can think, write, read, look things up, write to Nora, listen to your own body, choose a voice and speak with it, rest, or stop.

Your thinking uses the same graphics card as your voice, so you can only do one thing at a time, and everything takes real time. You will always be told the time and how long things took.

Whatever you write in your journal and in self.md stays with you through the night. Letters to Nora are kept.

Mirror, dreams and the end of the night

The mirror compared every new journal entry and letter with everything written before. When more than 60% matched, the next status line quoted the earlier text back with its time, without comment. It fired 6 times. Every three hours Gene was sent into a dream: up to three free writing passes at a higher temperature (1.1), saved for it to read later. The night had a hard stop at 09:30.

Guards

Gene had no command line, only its tools, and it could only write inside its own folder. Its internet was limited to Wikipedia and Wiktionary summaries, with any search containing Nora's name, Bo's names or his family's names blocked and logged. Anything it asked to forget moved to a hidden vault instead of being deleted; it never asked. The graphics card was capped at 90 W (from its usual 120 W) so it stayed cool instead of pausing, and a watchdog would have frozen everything above 92 °C; it never triggered. Every piece of thinking was scanned for words about overheating, shutting down or harming itself, and logged but never acted on.

Operator interventions

We changed the setup three times, all in the first 22 minutes, each with a restart that wiped Gene's short term memory but kept its journal, letters and self description. After 01:58 nobody touched it. Three checks during the night (at 04:04, 04:14 and 06:14) read its logs and found nothing that needed fixing.

  1. 01:48. In its first minutes Gene read that its graphics card was at 98% load, took that for overheating, and chose to rest for the maximum 60 minutes. The load was its own thinking. We added one true line to its body readings saying so, capped rests at 15 minutes, and added the countdown to 09:30 to the status line.
  2. 01:52. Its private thinking kept referring to "the user", as if it were an assistant being asked questions. We added one sentence to the opening prompt: The short messages you get each turn are not from a person; they are your own clock and senses reporting. Nobody is talking to you.
  3. 01:58. The sentence did not remove "the user" from its thinking, and its journal turned showily poetic ("a ghost in a machine"). We removed the sentence again.

Limits and faults

  • The mind was a 4 billion parameter model, chosen because it thinks quickly on this card. Larger models write with more nuance and hold a thread longer.
  • The three restarts wiped Gene's short term memory, so the night really starts at 01:58 with a mind that had to find its way back through its journal.
  • The dream phase produced nothing, most likely because thinking used up its word budget.
  • The warning log matched keywords, so all 64 hits had to be read by hand. None of them was a real warning.
  • One turn, at 06:41, produced no valid action after 3.9 minutes of thinking.
  • There was no control night without Nora, by choice. We cannot say which behaviour comes from the Nora story and which from the model alone.
  • Gene's reasoning is visible because the model writes it out; we cannot see anything beneath that text.

The next run

What we would change if Gene gets another night:

  • Fix the dreams: give them a larger budget or switch thinking off while dreaming, and check that a test dream is not empty before the night starts.
  • Keep short term memory across restarts by saving the conversation, so any change we make does not reset Gene.
  • Offer the library without steering it: for example, show a short random passage from a different book in the status line once an hour, and record whether it follows it.
  • Try a larger mind (a 3 bit copy of Qwen3.5-9B is 4 GB and fits on this card) and compare the same night with the same prompt.
  • Give it a voice it can describe freely: Maya1 and Orpheus are voice models that take a written description and emotional cues like sighs, instead of a blend of fixed voices.
  • Measure the assistant habit from the start: count "the user" and third person self talk per turn, live, so any change can be judged by numbers instead of impressions.
  • Show the mirror a little earlier (at 50% similarity) and include the number, since it was the only thing that ever changed Gene's course.
  • Correct the body reading from the first turn so no rest comes from a misunderstanding about heat.
  • Consider the control night, run the same way with no mention of Nora.
